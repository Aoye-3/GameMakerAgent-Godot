# Gameplay Lens

Use this lens for the game the player repeatedly performs, the decisions inside that activity, and the systems that shape mastery over time.

## Start From the Intended Experience

Identify:

- the player fantasy or emotion being promised;
- the primary verb and the shortest repeatable loop;
- the state change that tells the player the action mattered;
- the reason to repeat the loop after the first success.

Work backward from the desired experience to dynamics and rules. Do not add systems merely because they are conventional for the genre.

## Evaluation Axes

Use only the axes relevant to the question:

- **Action quality:** Is the primary action legible, responsive, and satisfying before progression or content rewards?
- **Meaningful decisions:** Do viable options have different consequences, or is one choice obviously dominant?
- **Feedback:** Can the player understand what happened, why it happened, and what to try next?
- **Challenge and mastery:** Does challenge reveal learnable patterns and allow skill growth rather than demanding guesswork?
- **Failure and recovery:** Is failure cost proportional to how often failure occurs? Can the player re-enter the interesting decision quickly?
- **Loop connection:** Do moment-to-moment actions support short goals, session goals, and progression, or do the layers pull in different directions?
- **System interactions:** Do mechanics create useful combinations, tradeoffs, or emergent behavior without producing a degenerate strategy?
- **Tuning leverage:** Which small set of variables actually changes feel, pacing, or strategy?

## Common Failure Patterns

- The fantasy is attractive, but the repeated verb is dull.
- Complexity adds rules without adding decisions.
- Rewards carry an action that is not satisfying on its own.
- Failure is readable only after an explanation outside the game.
- Progression removes interesting tradeoffs by making one route mandatory.
- Balance discussion starts with numbers before defining the intended behavior.
- A proposed feature solves boredom by adding content instead of strengthening the loop.

## Advisory Output

Prefer a recommendation that identifies:

1. the experience to preserve;
2. the highest-risk assumption in the loop;
3. one rule, interaction, or feedback change worth testing;
4. an observable playtest signal such as choice distribution, retry behavior, comprehension, or successful pattern learning.

Do not turn the recommendation into implementation architecture. If exact formulas are necessary, first establish their intended gameplay effect and safe tuning range.
