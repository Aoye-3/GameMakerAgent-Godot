# Experience Lens

Use this lens for what the player perceives, understands, controls, and feels while moving through the game.

## Follow the Player Journey

Examine the relevant slice of the journey rather than auditing every screen:

- entry and first impression;
- learning the goal and controls;
- reading state during real play;
- acting and receiving feedback;
- failure, recovery, pause, and return;
- completion and the next clear choice.

## Evaluation Axes

- **Goal clarity:** Can the player tell what matters now without reading a design explanation?
- **Affordance and mapping:** Do controls and interactive objects suggest what they do? Does the input mapping match the player's mental model?
- **Information priority:** Is the most urgent information visible under motion, pressure, and visual effects?
- **Feedback coherence:** Do visual, audio, haptic, animation, and state feedback agree about cause, timing, and severity?
- **Onboarding:** Is each new concept introduced close to the moment it becomes useful, with a safe chance to apply it?
- **Friction:** How many steps, waits, confirmations, or context switches separate intent from action?
- **Accessibility:** Can the critical interaction work without relying only on color, tiny text, audio, precise timing, or one input device?
- **Recovery:** After confusion or failure, does the game offer a clear next action without unnecessary repetition?
- **Aesthetic coherence:** Do art, audio, UI, writing, and motion reinforce the same player fantasy rather than compete for attention?

## Common Failure Patterns

- A tutorial explains controls but not the player's purpose.
- UI is readable in a static mockup but disappears during gameplay.
- Several feedback channels fire, yet none clearly identifies cause and effect.
- Accessibility is treated as a final settings screen instead of a property of the interaction.
- More text is proposed to solve a weak affordance.
- A striking visual direction reduces gameplay readability.
- The designer's familiarity is mistaken for first-time-player comprehension.

## Advisory Output

Anchor findings to a player moment. Prefer statements such as “When X happens, the player lacks Y signal, so Z interpretation is likely” over broad taste judgments.

Recommend the smallest experience change that can be observed in play. Useful signals include time to first successful action, repeated wrong inputs, missed state changes, hesitation, recovery time, and whether the player can explain the goal in their own words.

Do not prescribe UI code, asset production, or final art direction.
